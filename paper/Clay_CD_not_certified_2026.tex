\begin{abstract}
This is a negative-result note. It does \emph{not} solve, and makes no claim toward, the Clay Millennium Navier--Stokes problem. An earlier argument (its ``Section~10'') was proposed as a proof of finite-time blow-up, that is, of Fefferman's statements~(C) or~(D). It relied on a \emph{quadratic history clock} $\int_0^T\norm{\omega}_{\Linf}^2\dd t$ that diverges as $T\to\infty$. We explain exactly why this does not imply blow-up, and we machine-check the scalar real-analysis facts behind the explanation in Lean~4 with Mathlib:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em,itemsep=1pt]
\item a pointwise bound $f\le M$ on $[0,T]$ gives $\int_0^T f\le MT$;
\item if $f$ and $f^2$ are integrable on $[0,T]$, then $\int_0^T f\le \frac12\bigl(T+\int_0^T f^2\bigr)$, so a quadratic history that is finite on bounded intervals can never witness divergence of the Beale--Kato--Majda (BKM) integral at a finite time;
\item for constant $M\ne0$, $\int_0^T M^2\to+\infty$ as $T\to\infty$ while $\int_0^T M=MT$ for every finite $T$;
\item the model profile $(T^*-t)^{-1}$ and its square are not integrable on $[0,T^*]$, which is what genuine finite-time BKM divergence looks like.
\end{enumerate}
There are eight theorems, with no \texttt{sorry} and only Lean's standard axioms. No Navier--Stokes equation, vorticity field or BKM theorem is formalized; the BKM criterion is cited, not proved.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem statement that was attempted}
Fefferman's official formulation~\cite{fefferman} of the Navier--Stokes Millennium problem has four alternatives. (A) and (B) assert global smooth solutions on $\mathbb{R}^3$ and $\mathbb{T}^3$. (C) and (D) assert breakdown: there exist smooth, suitably decaying (respectively periodic) data and a smooth force for which no global smooth solution exists. A proof of (C) or (D) must therefore exhibit a solution whose smoothness is lost at some \emph{finite} time $T^*$.

\begin{theorem}[Beale--Kato--Majda~\cite{bkm}]\label{t:bkm}
If $T^*<\infty$ is the maximal time of existence of a smooth solution, then
\[
\int_0^{T^*}\norm{\omega(t)}_{\Linf}\dd t=\infty .
\]
\status{\Cited}
\end{theorem}

So a blow-up proof must show that the BKM integral diverges on a \emph{bounded} interval.

The Section~10 materials instead analysed a regime (2D, or a steady-Euler ``kill switch'') in which $M(t)\coloneqq\norm{\omega(t)}_{\Linf}$ stays bounded, and observed that the quadratic history $H(T)=\int_0^T M^2\dd t$ grows without bound as $T\to\infty$. A request to title the repository as a resolution of (C)/(D), and to present that argument as a zero-\texttt{sorry} proof, was declined. This note records why, with the reasons machine-checked.

\section{What is formally verified}
Throughout, $f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is an arbitrary real function of time standing in for $t\mapsto\norm{\omega(t)}_{\Linf}$, and integrals are Mathlib's Lebesgue interval integrals. Unless stated otherwise, declarations live in \lean{lean/BKMCalculus.lean}, namespace \lean{ClayCD.BKMCalculus}.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{bkm_integral_le_of_bound}]\label{t:bound}
If $0\le T$, $f$ is integrable on $[0,T]$ and $f(t)\le M$ for $t\in[0,T]$, then $\int_0^T f\le MT$.
\status{\Proved}
\end{theorem}
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem bkm_integral_le_of_bound {f : $\mathbb{R}$ $\to$ $\mathbb{R}$} {M T : $\mathbb{R}$} (hT : 0 $\le$ T)
    (hf : IntervalIntegrable f volume 0 T) (hb : $\forall$ t $\in$ Icc 0 T, f t $\le$ M) :
    $\int$ t in (0:$\mathbb{R}$)..T, f t $\le$ M * T
\end{lstlisting}
Hence in the bounded kill-switch regime the BKM integral is finite on every bounded interval, and Theorem~\ref{t:bkm} gives \emph{continuation}, the opposite of blow-up.

\begin{lemma}[\lean{le_half_one_add_sq}]\label{l:amgm}
For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, $x\le\frac12(1+x^2)$.
\status{\Proved}
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{bkm_integral_le_of_history}]\label{t:hist}
If $0\le T$ and $f$, $f^2$ are integrable on $[0,T]$, then
\[
\int_0^T f\dd t\;\le\; \frac12\Bigl(T+\int_0^T f^2\dd t\Bigr).
\]
\status{\Proved}
\end{theorem}
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem bkm_integral_le_of_history {f : $\mathbb{R}$ $\to$ $\mathbb{R}$} {T : $\mathbb{R}$} (hT : 0 $\le$ T)
    (hf : IntervalIntegrable f volume 0 T)
    (hf2 : IntervalIntegrable (fun t => f t ^ 2) volume 0 T) :
    $\int$ t in (0:$\mathbb{R}$)..T, f t $\le$ (T + $\int$ t in (0:$\mathbb{R}$)..T, f t ^ 2) / 2
\end{lstlisting}
This is the central negative result. If the history clock $H$ is finite at every finite time, as it is in the Section~10 regime, then the BKM integral is finite at every finite time. A history clock can detect a finite-time singularity only if it is \emph{itself} infinite on a bounded interval. Divergence ``at $T=\infty$'' carries no information about (C) or (D).

\begin{theorem}[\lean{history_diverges_at_infinity_bkm_finite}]\label{t:inf}
For $M\ne0$, $T\mapsto\int_0^T M^2\dd t$ tends to $+\infty$ as $T\to\infty$, and $\int_0^T M\dd t=MT$ for every $T$.
\status{\Proved}
\end{theorem}
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem history_diverges_at_infinity_bkm_finite (M : $\mathbb{R}$) (hM : M $\ne$ 0) :
    Tendsto (fun T : $\mathbb{R}$ => $\int$ _t in (0:$\mathbb{R}$)..T, M ^ 2) atTop atTop $\wedge$
    $\forall$ T : $\mathbb{R}$, $\int$ _t in (0:$\mathbb{R}$)..T, M = M * T
\end{lstlisting}
This is the explicit counterexample to the inference ``$H(T)\to\infty\Rightarrow$ blow-up''. It comes from a bounded, constant $M$, for which Theorem~\ref{t:bkm} gives global continuation.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{blowup_profile_not_integrable}, \lean{blowup_profile_sq_not_integrable}]\label{t:prof}
For $T^*>0$, neither $t\mapsto(T^*-t)^{-1}$ nor $t\mapsto(T^*-t)^{-2}$ is integrable on $[0,T^*]$.
\status{\Proved}
\end{theorem}
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem blowup_profile_not_integrable {Ts : $\mathbb{R}$} (hTs : 0 < Ts) :
    $\neg$ IntervalIntegrable (fun t : $\mathbb{R}$ => (Ts - t)$^{-1}$) volume 0 Ts
theorem blowup_profile_sq_not_integrable {Ts : $\mathbb{R}$} (hTs : 0 < Ts) :
    $\neg$ IntervalIntegrable (fun t : $\mathbb{R}$ => ((Ts - t)$^{-1}$) ^ 2) volume 0 Ts
\end{lstlisting}
This is the shape a genuine blow-up proof would need. The second statement is derived from the first through the pointwise bound $\lvert g\rvert\le\frac12(1+g^2)$, the same mechanism as Theorem~\ref{t:hist}.

\begin{remark}
The two definitional identities of version~1 are retained in \lean{lean/NotBlowup.lean} (namespace \lean{ClayCD.NotBlowup}): \lean{bounded_M_keeps_bkm_finite} and \lean{history_can_grow_while_bkm_stays_finite}. They hold by \texttt{rfl} (\Proved) and are subsumed by Theorems~\ref{t:bound} and~\ref{t:inf}, which work with actual integrals.
\end{remark}

\section{The precise gap: why this is not a proof of (C) or (D)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Wrong time scale.} (C)/(D) need divergence on $[0,T^*]$ with $T^*<\infty$. The Section~10 quantity diverges only as $T\to\infty$ (Theorems~\ref{t:hist} and~\ref{t:inf}).
\item \textbf{Wrong direction.} Bounded $M$ is exactly the hypothesis under which BKM \emph{continues} solutions (Theorem~\ref{t:bound}).
\item \textbf{Wrong dimension and regime.} The 2D and steady-Euler regimes are classically globally regular, so they cannot carry a counterexample to 3D regularity.
\item \textbf{No PDE is formalized.} No equation, solution, function space or BKM theorem appears in the Lean code. The lemmas are scalar calculus; their relevance to Navier--Stokes passes through the \emph{cited} Theorem~\ref{t:bkm}.
\item \textbf{Forced (C)/(D) claims elsewhere.} An external claim exists in the repository\\ \path{openai/NavierStokesAndEuler}. It is neither copied nor endorsed here.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Lessons and negative results}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item A monotone ``clock'' functional that is finite at every finite time is not a blow-up witness. Theorem~\ref{t:hist} turns this into a one-line test: check whether the clock can be infinite on a \emph{bounded} interval.
\item Formalizing closed forms by definition (version~1) checks nothing. Lemmas about real integrals (version~2) at least check the calculus the argument relies on.
\item A build and axiom audit shows only that the stated theorems follow from the standard axioms. It is not a certificate, and it says nothing about whether the prose reading is the intended one.
\end{itemize}
